<objective>
Prove the whole Phase 2 architecture end to end on one path before any of it is built out.

By the end of this plan a person can put on a headset, open the page, tap the orb, hear the agent
say beat A's scripted line in a real synthesised voice, answer it out loud, and watch one canned
tool call move the page from frame A to frame B. Every layer the phase will touch is wired: the
server-side key mint, the WebRTC transport, the beat table, the beat runner, the tool handler seam,
and the component. Nothing is stubbed in a way that would need an architectural change to fill in.

Task 1 is a mechanical state extraction that has to land before the transport can drive the page —
it is the seam, not a horizontal layer, and it is deliberately first because the three animation
regressions it can cause are invisible to a type-check and much harder to attribute once voice is
also in the picture.

Purpose: the WebRTC transport is the riskiest and least reversible part of this phase. If the
handshake, the verbatim mechanism or the VAD silence contract do not work, we need to know after
one commit, not after ten.

Output: a live, speaking orb on one beat, plus the module skeleton plans 02-02 and 02-03 expand.

`type SessionPhase = "idle" | "connecting" | "live" | "scripted"`,

## Handoff to Phase 4 (recording checklist)

Two UI-SPEC rows resolve to a human rehearsal obligation rather than code. They belong in the shoot
checklist, not in this plan's tasks:

1. **Do one silent orb tap before rolling.** The browser's native mic-permission dialog cannot be
   suppressed by the component; granting the origin beforehand is the only way it never appears on
   camera.
2. **Record in a non-16:9 window.** The operator overlays live in the letterbox/pillarbox margin
   outside the scaled stage. That margin exists only when the viewport's aspect ratio differs from
   16:9 — scale is irrelevant. `2560x1440` scales to 1.333 with **zero** margin; `1366x768` scales
   to 0.711 with only 0.3 px. `1512x982`, `1440x900`, `1280x800` and `1024x640` all give a usable
   band. The runtime check is
   `innerHeight - 1080 * Math.min(innerWidth/1920, innerHeight/1080) >= N`, evaluated live, with the
   horizontal analogue `innerWidth - 1920 * Math.min(...)`. Never infer the band from a scale value.
