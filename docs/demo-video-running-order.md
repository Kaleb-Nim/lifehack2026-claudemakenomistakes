Merchant-first is the right order: the audience learns what a listing *is* before they watch one get found. Every attribute the shopper filters on in segment 2 — model number, ANC, same-day east-side collection, parallel-import warranty — was visibly created in segment 1.

## The seam

Segment 1 ends at ~2:10 on a **simulated test query**: *"noise cancelling earbuds under 200, collect today, east side"* → **first of seven**.

**Segment 2 must not replay that query.** Repeating it makes the ending of segment 1 redundant and wastes the strongest asset the ordering gives us. Instead:

**Open segment 2 on a vaguer ask from a real shopper** — something like *"need earbuds for my commute, around 200"* — and let the agent run the clarifying loop the Visa mentor explicitly asked for (she used the flight example: when, where, full-service vs. budget). One or two narrowing questions — noise cancelling or not? collecting today or happy to wait? — and the search resolves onto **Hock Seng**, the merchant we just watched onboard.

The payoff is that the shop from segment 1 wins the sale in segment 2. That is a story rather than a recap.

## Three things segment 1 hands segment 2

The consumer script must pick these up, or segment 1's promises go unpaid:

1. **The standing rules must appear at checkout.** Phase 7 had the merchant set: price firm at $149, 7-day return with complete box, no returns on opened earbuds. The agent promised to state all of it *before* payment. Show that.
2. **The parallel-import disclosure must be stated before the shopper pays.** The agent's line in segment 1 was *"Hiding it starts arguments — saying it sells better."* If the shopper pays without seeing it, we contradicted ourselves on camera.
3. **The merchant notification must fire.** At 2:03 the agent told the merchant *"every sale I close, you'll know the moment it happens."* **End segment 2 on that notification landing on the merchant's phone.**

Item 3 is the strongest structural move available. It closes the loop back to segment 1, pays off the auto-transact decision (no per-sale approval, but instant visibility), and gives the whole video an ending rather than a stop.

## Don't double up on Visa

Segment 1 ends on **Visa payout setup** (merchant getting paid). Segment 2 ends on **Visa checkout** (shopper paying). Two Visa moments is correct and shows both sides of the payments stack — but they must look and feel different. Payout setup is a one-time connection; checkout is a transaction. Different screens, different language.
